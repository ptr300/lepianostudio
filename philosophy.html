Teaching philosophy

As a teacher, my priority for students is to develop a sense of expression and musicianship. That is, they connect with music in a more intimate manner and express that connection in their own playing. I want to share my own passion and cultivate a lifelong love of music within them. As a result of my instruction, students will not only be learning the piano and how to play notes but also how to make it beautiful and create their own voice from the instrument.

Above all else, I strive to maintain a positive learning environment. I want them to enjoy taking lessons and know that they are in a safe space. A big part of that involves instilling both hard work and fun in each lesson. This involves applying concepts to engaging activities that keep the students interested. I will always remain honest and constructive but also encouraging in my feedback. A moment in which a student is negatively impacted by my words or actions would be a great failure for me as a teacher.

I believe a student learns best when they work towards short-term goals that sufficiently challenge them. This will allow them to accomplish the goal and grow from it without it being overly difficult or easy to the point that they would gain nothing/become bored. If a piece is beyond one’s technical abilities, then the student would only become frustrated at their shortcomings and even injure themselves attempting to perform certain skills in some cases. In addition to technical and stylistic knowledge a student may gain from the repertoire, it must also interest them; enjoying what they play greatly motivates students. To foster this kind of learning, I use my judgment and understanding of piano repertoire to take students to the next level by appropriate increments so that I retain motivation and limit frustration.

All the work that I do with my students should result in the development of independent learners who are able to take the initiative into their own hands. I believe that the earlier they can start to think for themselves, the quicker they will grow as musicians. In lessons, I will challenge my students and ask them to listen to themselves and evaluate: what went well, and what could be improved. Furthermore, I will pair that with detailed work to do at home and how to effectively practice so that they can solve problems independently. Listening to professional recordings is another great tool for students to understand the difference between a mediocre sound and a beautiful sound. This can be done both during lessons - in which I can guide them to hear intricate details - and at home, where they can hear many different interpretations and decide what they like the most. It is also important to understand what is happening in the music; this means that I introduce music theory to all my students.

I will use these deep-rooted beliefs to continue my growth as a teacher and guide me so that I can provide students with a valuable music experience. I strive to refine my craft and adapt to whatever changes may happen in the future. I have been taught and raised by wonderful teachers and only wish to pass down that opportunity to my own students in a similar manner.
